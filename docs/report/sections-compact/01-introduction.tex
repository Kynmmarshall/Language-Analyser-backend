\section{Introduction}\label{c:intro}

\subsection{Context and objective}

Yaound\'e is a multilingual city. In one taxi ride a passenger may hear French grammar
carrying English verbs, a Pidgin discourse particle and a noun borrowed from Duala or
Ewondo --- often inside a single sentence. The resulting variety, \emph{Francanglais}, has
no standard written form, no authoritative dictionary and no fixed orthography, yet it is
the working language of commuting, market bargaining, roadside trade and campus life.

\textbf{Objective.} To design and implement a mini-language analyzer performing lexical
and syntactic analysis on real spoken expressions collected in Yaound\'e, and to detect
patterns relating to commuting, market activity, security, weather, transport,
electricity and everyday transactions.

\textbf{Scope.} A compiler \emph{front end}: scanner, lexer, grammar preparation, LL(1)
table construction and a table-driven predictive parser, delivered as a web application.
Semantic analysis, code generation and optimisation are out of scope, as are machine
translation and statistical language modelling --- every decision the analyzer makes is
rule-based and traceable to a specific lexicon entry or grammar production.

\begin{keypoint}[Guiding design principle]
The analyzer recognises a \emph{documented subset} of collected speech. It never pretends
to understand every sentence, never silently discards an unknown word, and never silently
resolves a grammar ambiguity. Unknown vocabulary and parsing conflicts are both reported
explicitly, because those reports are the evidence this report is built on.
\end{keypoint}
