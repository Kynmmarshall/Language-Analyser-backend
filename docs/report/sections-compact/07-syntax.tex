\section{Syntactic Analysis}\label{c:syntax}

\subsection{What the grammar has to describe}

Real Yaound\'e speech does not arrive as tidy subject--verb--object sentences. The grammar
must admit clauses juxtaposed without any conjunction; a fronted prepositional phrase
before the subject; bare interjections and frozen formulas standing alone as complete
utterances; serial verbs with no linking word; pre-verbal negation and adverbs interleaved
inside the verb group; and subject dropping, leaving a bare predicate. All six occur in the
collected data.

\nonterm{Seq} $\rightarrow$ \nonterm{Seq} \term{CONJ} \nonterm{Unit} is the natural way to
say ``a statement is a list of units joined by conjunctions or separators'', and three of
its four alternatives recurse on the left. \nonterm{ClauseTail} $\rightarrow$ \nonterm{NP}
\nonterm{Predicate} covers left-dislocated topics (\fw{le taximan, lui il sait}), which are
common and would otherwise be rejected. \nonterm{VerbTail} is the serial-verb device: it
lets verbs, adverbs and negations chain after the head verb without bounding the number of
them.

\subsubsection*{Step 1 --- left-recursion elimination on \nonterm{Seq}}

Applying the standard construction, the immediately left-recursive
\nonterm{Seq} $\rightarrow$ \nonterm{Seq}$\,\alpha_i \mid \beta$ becomes:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\quad}l@{}}
\nonterm{Seq}    & $\rightarrow$ \nonterm{Unit} \nonterm{Seq$'$} \\
\nonterm{Seq$'$} & $\rightarrow$ \term{CONJ} \nonterm{Unit} \nonterm{Seq$'$}
                   $\mid$ \term{SEP} \nonterm{Unit} \nonterm{Seq$'$}
                   $\mid$ \term{SEP} \term{CONJ} \nonterm{Unit} \nonterm{Seq$'$}
                   $\mid$ $\varepsilon$ \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsubsection*{Steps 2--4 --- left factoring}

Elimination immediately \emph{creates} a new conflict: two \nonterm{Seq$'$} alternatives now
begin with \term{SEP}, so a predictive parser cannot choose between them on one token of
lookahead. That is factored into \nonterm{Seq$''$}. Two pre-existing conflicts are factored
the same way.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{\hskip 26pt}l@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Nonterminal} & \textbf{Common prefix} & \textbf{Fresh} & \textbf{Result} \\
\midrule
\nonterm{Seq$'$} & \term{SEP}  & \nonterm{Seq$''$} & \nonterm{Seq$''$} $\rightarrow$ \nonterm{Unit} \nonterm{Seq$'$} $\mid$ \term{CONJ} \nonterm{Unit} \nonterm{Seq$'$} \\
\nonterm{Unit}   & \term{INTJ} & \nonterm{Unit$'$} & \nonterm{Unit$'$} $\rightarrow$ \nonterm{Clause} $\mid$ $\varepsilon$ \\
\nonterm{NP}     & \term{DET}  & \nonterm{NP$'$}   & \nonterm{NP$'$} $\rightarrow$ \term{NOUN} $\mid$ \term{ADJ} \term{NOUN} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\nonterm{NP} is the clearest illustration: \nonterm{NP} $\rightarrow$ \term{DET} \term{NOUN}
and \nonterm{NP} $\rightarrow$ \term{DET} \term{ADJ} \term{NOUN} share \term{DET}, so the
decision must be delayed until after the determiner is consumed.

\begin{keypoint}[Order matters]
Left factoring is applied \emph{after} left-recursion elimination, and repeatedly until no
common prefixes remain. Running them in the other order would leave the \term{SEP} conflict
that elimination introduces, and the grammar would not be LL(1).
\end{keypoint}

\subsection{FIRST and FOLLOW sets}

FIRST and FOLLOW are computed by fixed-point iteration: repeatedly apply the standard
closure rules until no set changes. Nullable nonterminals are those whose FIRST derivation
reaches $\varepsilon$; here they are \nonterm{ClauseOpt}, \nonterm{ClauseTail},
\nonterm{Comps}, \nonterm{NegOpt}, \nonterm{Seq$'$}, \nonterm{Unit$'$} and
\nonterm{VerbTail}.

\subsection{LL(1) parsing table}

The table is built by the standard rule: for each production $A \rightarrow \alpha$, add it
at $[A, t]$ for every $t \in \mathrm{FIRST}(\alpha)$; and if $\alpha$ is nullable, also for
every $t \in \mathrm{FOLLOW}(A)$. A second production landing in an occupied cell is a
conflict.

\begin{keypoint}[Result: conflict-free]
The constructed table contains \textbf{no duplicated cells}. The grammar is LL(1): the
parser needs exactly one token of lookahead, with no backtracking and no ambiguity
resolution heuristics. \texttt{tests/test\_ll1.py} asserts this property, so a future
grammar edit that introduces a conflict fails the test suite rather than degrading silently
(\req{NFR-3}).
\end{keypoint}

Rows are nonterminals, columns are terminals plus the end marker \term{\$}, and each cell
holds the production to apply. Blank cells are exactly where a syntax error is detected.
