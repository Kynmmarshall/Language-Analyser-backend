\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Project context}

Yaound\'e is a multilingual city. In a single taxi ride a passenger may hear French
grammar carrying English verbs, a Pidgin discourse particle, and a noun borrowed from
Duala or Ewondo --- often inside one sentence. The resulting variety, commonly called
\emph{Francanglais} or \emph{camfranglais}, has no standard written form, no authoritative
dictionary, and no fixed orthography. It is nonetheless the everyday working language of
commuting, market bargaining, roadside trade and campus life.

\subsection{Objective}

To design and implement a mini-language analyzer capable of performing lexical and
syntactic analysis on real spoken expressions collected in Yaound\'e, and to detect
patterns relating to commuting, market activity, security, weather, transport, electricity
and everyday transactions.

\subsection{Scope}

\textbf{In scope.} Manual collection and transcription of real statements; a hand-written
scanner and lexer driven by documented regular expressions; a context-free grammar reduced
to LL(1) form with recorded transformations; FIRST/FOLLOW computation and parsing-table
construction; a predictive parser returning an explicit accept/reject verdict; token
frequency and variation analysis; and a web application exposing all of the above.

\textbf{Out of scope.} Semantic analysis, code generation and optimisation --- this is a
compiler \emph{front end}. Machine translation, speech recognition and statistical
language modelling are likewise excluded: every decision the analyzer makes is rule-based
and traceable to a specific lexicon entry or grammar production.

\begin{keypoint}[Guiding design principle]
The analyzer recognises a \emph{documented subset} of collected speech. It never pretends
to understand every sentence, never silently discards an unknown word, and never silently
resolves a grammar ambiguity. Unknown vocabulary and parsing conflicts are both reported
explicitly, because those reports are the evidence this report is built on.
\end{keypoint}
